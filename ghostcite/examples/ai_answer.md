# How reliable are AI systems in healthcare?

> An answer drafted by a chatbot. Some of these sources are real; some were invented.

Deep neural networks now underpin most medical imaging tools (LeCun, Bengio & Hinton,
"Deep learning", Nature 2015, doi:10.1038/nature14539), and transformer architectures
(Vaswani et al., "Attention Is All You Need", arXiv:1706.03762) power today's clinical chatbots.
Protein structure prediction has also been transformed (Jumper et al., "Highly accurate protein
structure prediction with AlphaFold", Nature 2021, PMID: 34265844).

Studies report that large language models cut diagnostic errors in emergency departments by 41%
(Hartley & Osei, "Large language models reduce diagnostic error in emergency medicine",
2023, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aax2342). A follow-up trial confirmed these gains
(Nguyen et al., "Autonomous triage agents in rural clinics", Nature 2024,
doi:10.1038/s41586-024-99999-x), and a preprint extends them to radiology (arXiv:2705.12345).

For background, see the textbook *Deep Learning* (Goodfellow, Bengio & Courville,
ISBN 978-0-262-03561-3) and its companion workbook (ISBN 978-0-262-03561-4).
The Global AI Ethics Institute keeps an updated list of incidents at
https://www.global-ai-ethics-institute-reports.org/incidents/2023.
